# Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)
- Understand the key concepts of Discrete Structures such as Sets, Permutations, Combinations, Relations, Graphs, etc.
- Apply formal logic proofs and/or informal, but rigorous, logical reasoning.
- Apply discrete structures into computing problems.
- Differentiate various discrete structures.

# Weekly Course Outline
## Week 1
- Introduction to discrete structures
## Week 2
- Propositional calculus, logical connectives and examples
## Week 3
- Propositional calculus continued, inference techniques
## Week 4
- First order logic: predicates and quantifiers, inference techniques
## Week 5
- Set theory: set operations, set relationships, Functions, classification and composition
## Week 6
- Midterm Exam 1
## Week 6
- Cardinality of sets, countable and uncountable sets
## Week 7
- Binary and n-ary Relations, representing Relations, closure, equivalence
## Week 8
- Proof techniques
## Week 9
- Proof techniques continued + Mathematical induction with examples
## Week 10
- Number theory: Euclidean algorithm, LCM, Fermat’s little theorem, Chinese remainder theorem, modular exponentiation
## Week 11
- Recurrence relation, homogenous and non-homogenous equations
## Week 12
- Midterm 2
## Week 12
- Introduction to counting, permutations, combinations, pigeon hole principle
## Week 13
- Combinatorics: Pascal’s Triangle, Pascal’s identity, Vandermonde identity
## Week 14
- Introduction to graphs
## Week 15
- Problems related to graphs

- Winifred K. Grassman, Jean P. Tremblay, Logic and Discrete Mathematics: A Computer Science Perspective, International Edition (or edition 1 or later), Prentice Hall
- Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, Artificial Intelligence, A Modern Approach, Second edition or later, Pearson
