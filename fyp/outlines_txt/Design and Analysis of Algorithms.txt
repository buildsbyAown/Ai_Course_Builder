# Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)
- Explain what is meant by “best”, “expected”, and “worst” case behavior of an algorithm
- Identify the characteristics of data and/or other conditions or assumptions that lead to different behaviors
- Determine informally the time and space complexity of simple algorithms
- List and contrast standard complexity classes
- Use big O, Omega, Theta notation formally to give asymptotic upper bounds on time and space complexity of algorithms
- Use of the strategies (brute-force, greedy, divide-and-conquer, and dynamic programming) to solve an appropriate problem
- Solve problems using graph algorithms, including single-source and all-pairs shortest paths, and at least one minimum spanning tree algorithm

# Weekly Course Outline
## Week 1
- The role of algorithms in computers, Asymptotic functions and notations (Big-oh, big-omega, theta), best and worst case time complexity
## Week 2-4
- Divide and Conquer (maximum subarray sum, counting inversions, quicksort, merge sort) + Solving recurrences
## Week 5
- Lower bound for comparison based sorting, Sorting in linear time: Count Sort
## Week 6-7
- Dynamic Programming (maximum subarray, rod cutting, longest common subsequence, binary knapsack)
## Week 8-9
- Greedy Algorithms (Activity selection, fractional knapsack and Huffman codes)
## Week 10
- Introduction to graphs (revision of BFS, DFS) and their application (Cycle Detection, shortest paths, connected components etc.)
## Week 11
- Applications of DFS (topological sort, strongly connected components)
## Week 12
- Minimum Spanning Trees (MST) (Prim's Algorithm and Kruskal's Algorithm)
## Week 13-14
- Shortest Path Algorithms (Dijkstra's Algorithm, Bellman-Ford and Floyd Warshall Algorithm)
